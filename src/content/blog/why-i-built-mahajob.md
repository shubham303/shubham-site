---
title: "Why I built mahajob.in, a job board for local hiring"
date: 2026-07-11
description: "The unglamorous corner of India's job market that the big portals forgot, and why AI finally made it worth solving."
---

In India, if you are hiring a software engineer, you have Naukri, LinkedIn, Indeed and a
dozen others. If you are a small business in a Tier-2 town looking for a shop assistant, a
delivery rider, an electrician, a receptionist or a factory hand, you have almost nothing
built for you.

That is the gap. The everyday world of **SME, blue-collar and grey-collar jobs** has no
portal that fits it. Most of the people looking for those jobs are **not tech-savvy**. They
are not comfortable on Naukri or Indeed, which were designed for white-collar professionals
uploading polished PDFs.

So companies hire through **employment agencies** instead. Agencies charge the employer a
hefty fee, often charge the *job seeker* too, and are riddled with scams and middlemen who
take money and deliver nothing. The person who can least afford it pays the most, for the
least trust.

No one fixed this because the segment is **low margin**. The players big enough to build it
have no reason to, and the people who need it cannot build it themselves. It sat there,
unglamorous and ignored.

Then **AI made building the thing cheap.** A focused website connecting job seekers with
employers across Maharashtra once needed a team and a budget. Now one person can build and
run it. So I did.

[**mahajob.in**](https://www.mahajob.in/) is that site. Employers post jobs, job seekers
apply, and it is **free for everyone**. No agency fee, no charging the person looking for
work. It is slowly gaining traction: real jobs get posted, real people apply.

None of this is glamorous. It is not a frontier model or a clever paper. It is a plain
website solving a plain problem for people the tech industry usually forgets, and that is
the kind of problem I want to spend my time on. mahajob.in is my first real crack at it. It
will not be the last.
