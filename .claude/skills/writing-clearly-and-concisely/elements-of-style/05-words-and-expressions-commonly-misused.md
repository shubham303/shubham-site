# V. Words and Expressions Commonly Misused

**All right.** Two words. Reserve for the sense "agreed" or "go ahead".

**Alternate / alternative.** *Alternate* means every other one in a series;
*alternative* means one of two or more choices.

**Among / between.** *Between* is for two, or for more than two when the
relationship is individual and reciprocal. *Among* is for indefinite groups.

**And/or.** A device that damages a sentence and often leads to ambiguity. Write
the two options out.

**Anticipate.** Use *expect* in the sense of simple expectation. *Anticipate*
means to forestall or act in advance.

**As to whether.** *Whether* is sufficient.

**Case.** Often unnecessary. "In many cases the rooms were poorly ventilated"
-> "Many rooms were poorly ventilated."

**Certainly.** Used indiscriminately by some as an intensifier, it weakens
everything it touches. Use sparingly.

**Character.** Often redundant. "Acts of a hostile character" -> "hostile acts."

**Claim** (verb). With object-noun, means *lay claim to*. Not a substitute for
*declare*, *maintain*, or *charge*.

**Compare to / compare with.** *Compare to* points out a resemblance between
things of different order. *Compare with* examines two things of the same order
for likenesses or differences.

**Comprise.** The whole comprises the parts. Not "is comprised of".

**Data.** Historically plural. Modern technical usage as a mass noun ("the data
is") is widely accepted; be consistent within a document.

**Different than.** Prefer *different from*.

**Due to.** Correctly used as a predicate adjective ("The accident was due to
carelessness"). Loosely used for *through*, *because of*, or *owing to* in
adverbial phrases: "He lost the first game due to carelessness" -> "because of".

**Effect.** As a noun, *result*; as a verb, *to bring about*. Not *affect*, which
as a verb means *to influence*.

**Etc.** Not to be used of persons. Equivalent to *and the rest*, so *and etc.*
is redundant. Its use is legitimate only when at least two items have been
named and their inclusion in the class is clear.

**Fact.** Use only of matters capable of direct verification, not of judgments.
See the note on *the fact that* in Rule 13 — revise it out.

**Farther / further.** *Farther* for distance, *further* for degree or extent.

**Fewer / less.** *Fewer* for countable items, *less* for quantity or mass.

**However.** In the sense "nevertheless", it should not come first in the
sentence.

**Imply / infer.** The speaker implies; the listener infers.

**Interesting.** An announcement that what follows is interesting is a poor
substitute for making it so. Avoid "an interesting fact is that".

**Kind of / sort of.** Not to be used as substitutes for *rather* or *something
like*.

**Leverage.** As a verb, corporate padding. Use *use*.

**Literally.** Not a general intensifier. Use only when the statement is
literally true.

**Nature.** Often meaningless. "Acts of a hostile nature" -> "hostile acts."

**One of the most.** An empty formula; avoid beginning a piece of writing with it.

**Respective / respectively.** Usually removable without loss.

**Should / would.** Use *should* only for duty or condition, *would* for the
conditional.

**So.** Avoid so as an intensifier ("so good", "so beautiful") when no
consequence follows.

**State.** Not a substitute for *say* or *remark*. It means *express fully or
clearly*.

**Student body.** Cumbersome. Use *students*.

**System.** Frequently redundant. "The dormitory system" -> "the dormitories."

**Thanking you in advance.** Presumptuous. Write the thanks after the favor.

**The truth is / the fact is.** Padding. Delete.

**Utilize.** Use *use*.

**Very.** Use sparingly. Where emphasis is necessary, use words strong in
themselves.

**While.** Avoid using as a substitute for *and*, *but*, or *although*. Its
proper sense is *during the time that*.

**Whom.** Often used incorrectly for *who* before verbs of saying and thinking.

**Worth while.** Overworked as a term of vague approval. Say what makes it worth
doing.

**-ize.** Resist coining new verbs in *-ize* where a plain verb exists:
*operationalize*, *incentivize*, *architect* (as a verb).
