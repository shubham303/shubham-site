# IV. A Few Matters of Form

**Headings.** Leave a blank line after the title or heading. In a manuscript,
do not use a period after a title or heading. Keep heading levels consistent and
do not skip levels.

**Numerals.** Do not spell out dates or other serial numbers. Write them in
figures or in Roman notation.

    August 9, 1918        Chapter XII        Rule 3        352 West 55 Street

**Parentheses.** A sentence containing an expression in parenthesis is punctuated
outside the marks of parenthesis exactly as if the parenthetical expression were
absent. The expression within is punctuated as if it stood by itself, except
that the final stop is omitted unless it is a question mark or exclamation point.

    He declares (and no one denies it) that the charge is baseless.

**Quotations.** Formal quotations cited as documentary evidence are introduced
by a colon and enclosed in quotation marks. Quotations grammatically in
apposition, or the direct objects of verbs, are preceded by a comma and enclosed
in quotation marks. Quotations introduced by *that* are regarded as indirect and
take no quotation marks.

Proverbial expressions and familiar phrases of literary origin require no
quotation marks.

**References.** In scholarly work intended for careful study, give references in
parentheses or in footnotes, not in the body of the sentence. Omit the words
*act*, *scene*, *line*, *book*, *volume*, and *page* except when only one is
given. Use figures and lowercase Roman numerals.

**Titles.** For the titles of literary works, scholarly usage prefers italics
with capitalized initials. In ordinary writing, quotation marks are the common
alternative. Whichever you choose, use it consistently throughout a document.

**Syllabication.** When a word must be divided at the end of a line, divide it
according to its formation and pronunciation. Never divide a one-syllable word,
and never leave a single letter stranded.

## Applied to technical writing

- One idea per heading; the heading should predict what follows.
- Prefer prose to bullets when the items are related by argument rather than
  enumeration. Reserve bullets for genuine lists.
- Use code formatting for identifiers, paths, flags, and literal output. Do not
  bold ordinary words for emphasis.
- Do not decorate headings with emoji.
- Do not end a document with a summary that restates what the reader just read.
